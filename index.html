HEXLANDS

Lay the land tile by tile, then send your animals out to live in it.

Split tiles

Some tiles are split down the middle — three sides one terrain, three the other. A split tile only counts as a terrain for a neighbour that half is facing, so turning it decides what it is worth and to whom. The dot in each half says which is which.

Special tiles

Four tiles in the bag are special. Lay one and its animal steps straight onto it — no layout to satisfy, and it does not spend your animal move for the turn.

Options

Lay a tile. Draw a few from the bag and keep one — the rest go back and the bag is shuffled. The first tile can go anywhere; after that every tile must touch the land already on the board. You may also lay a tile over one already down, so long as no animal stands on it — reshaping the land and cutting or making connections. Drop it and it waits: turn it, confirm it, or send it back.
Then move one animal. Each card shows the layout of land needed to send that animal out, and the layout needed to call it home. Both score. Otherwise walk one animal a single tile, and wait.
The ladder. Early animals go out on land alone. Later ones need a particular animal already out beside them — a loon needs a fish to dive for, a bear a fish to catch. Coming home runs the other way: some animals just need the right land, others are driven home by something bigger, as a loon leaves the nest when a bear reaches the shore. Points climb with every rung.
